Antonino Costa · HKUST

The Impact of Politicians’ Personal Financial Disclosures on Connected Firms’ Voluntary Disclosure

Solo authored

Job Market Paper

This paper examines how the public disclosure of an individual–firm connection affects the firm’s voluntary financial disclosure. Italy’s personal financial disclosure mandate requires politicians of municipalities with more than 15,000 residents to publish their managerial appointments, asset holdings, and tax filings. These disclosures make connected firms identifiable and attribute their financial statements to the politician. Exploiting the mandate’s unexpected extension to incumbent politicians, I compare firms whose connections were exposed with firms connected to exempt politicians. Following the mandate, connected firms reduce voluntary financial disclosure. The reduction is confined to connections the mandate exposes for the first time, to municipalities where voters are attentive and attribute business income to public office, and to the tails of the firm performance distribution, where financial statements are most revealing. The pattern is consistent with politicians’ privacy cost of disclosure and inconsistent with signaling legitimacy or concealing rent extraction. Treated politicians become more likely to be covered in articles on their income, and those connected to a firm face worse reelection outcomes. Overall, the evidence suggests that transparency mandates directed at individuals generate information spillovers that make connected firms more opaque.

The Impact of Municipal-Auditor Appointments on Auditors’ Private-Sector Audit Engagements

with Zhihong Chen and Qingkai Dong

Conditionally accepted, Journal of Accounting Research

This paper examines how municipal-auditor appointments affect auditors’ private-sector audit engagements. Leveraging the lottery-based selection of municipal auditors in Italy, we compare first-time appointees with auditors who have never been appointed. We find that appointed auditors acquire more private clients and expand the geographic reach of their practices, although these clients are not necessarily located closer to the appointing municipalities. These effects are more pronounced for less experienced auditors and in more competitive audit markets. The increase in private clients appears to be primarily driven by greater visibility and networks with other municipal auditors, rather than connections with politicians. We also show that local municipal appointments motivate other auditors to become eligible for future drafts, as evidenced by an increase in new professional registrations. Overall, our findings highlight the role of professional experience gained through municipal-auditor service in shaping labor market outcomes within the auditing profession.
