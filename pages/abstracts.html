Research Abstracts

Antonino Costa · HKUST

Privacy Concerns and Private Firms’ Voluntary Disclosure: Evidence from Mandatory Personal Financial Disclosures

Solo authored

Job Market Paper

This paper examines whether making firms’ connections to individuals publicly visible affects firms’ voluntary disclosure. I exploit Italy’s mandatory personal financial disclosure mandate, which requires politicians to disclose managerial appointments in private firms alongside detailed personal financial information. Using a difference-in-differences design that exploits a population threshold determining which municipalities fall under the mandate, I find that firms connected to newly exposed politicians reduce voluntary financial disclosure following the mandate. The reduction occurs along both the extensive and intensive margins, persists beyond the politician’s term in office, and is concentrated among firms for which the mandate creates new attribution and where public attention and the potential informational content of firms’ financial statements are greater. Additional evidence indicates that the mandate increases media coverage of politicians’ personal finances and worsens their electoral outcomes, while the disclosure response does not depend on proxies for rent extraction. Overall, the findings show that mandatory disclosure imposed on individuals can reduce voluntary disclosure by connected firms by increasing the privacy costs of firms’ financial reporting.

The Impact of Municipal–Auditor Appointments on Auditors’ Private–Sector Audit Engagements

with Zhihong Chen and Qingkai Dong

Conditionally accepted, Journal of Accounting Research

This paper examines how municipal–auditor appointments affect auditors’ private–sector audit engagements. Leveraging the lottery–based selection of municipal auditors in Italy, we compare first–time appointees with auditors who have never been appointed. We find that appointed auditors acquire more private clients and expand the geographic reach of their practices, although these clients are not necessarily located closer to the appointing municipalities. These effects are more pronounced for less experienced auditors and in more competitive audit markets. The increase in private clients appears to be primarily driven by greater visibility and networks with other municipal auditors, rather than connections with politicians. We also show that local municipal appointments motivate other auditors to become eligible for future drafts, as evidenced by an increase in new professional registrations. Overall, our findings highlight the role of professional experience gained through municipal-auditor service in shaping labor market outcomes within the auditing profession.
