Research Abstracts

Antonino Costa · HKUST

Privacy Concerns and Private Firms’ Voluntary Disclosure: Evidence from Mandatory Personal Financial Disclosures

Solo authored

Job Market Paper

This paper examines how publicly attributing a firm to a specific individual affects the firm’s voluntary financial disclosure. Italy’s personal financial disclosure mandate requires politicians of municipalities with more than 15,000 residents to publish their managerial appointments, asset holdings, and tax filings. By publicly linking individuals to the firms they manage, the mandate changes the visibility of existing firm–individual connections. Exploiting the mandate's unexpected extension to incumbent politicians, I compare firms that became attributable with firms connected to exempt politicians. Treated firms reduce voluntary financial disclosure. The reduction is confined to firms the mandate makes attributable for the first time, firms in municipalities with greater public attention, and firms whose financial statements are most informative about the individual. The pattern is consistent with the individual’s privacy concerns about disclosure. The response persists where politicians have limited opportunities for rent extraction, indicating that the response does not require wrongdoing. Treated politicians become more likely to receive media coverage of their income, and those connected to firms experience worse reelection outcomes. Overall, the evidence suggests that enabling attribution heightens the privacy concern of individuals, who react by reducing their firms’ disclosure.

The Impact of Municipal–Auditor Appointments on Auditors’ Private–Sector Audit Engagements

with Zhihong Chen and Qingkai Dong

Conditionally accepted, Journal of Accounting Research

This paper examines how municipal–auditor appointments affect auditors’ private–sector audit engagements. Leveraging the lottery–based selection of municipal auditors in Italy, we compare first–time appointees with auditors who have never been appointed. We find that appointed auditors acquire more private clients and expand the geographic reach of their practices, although these clients are not necessarily located closer to the appointing municipalities. These effects are more pronounced for less experienced auditors and in more competitive audit markets. The increase in private clients appears to be primarily driven by greater visibility and networks with other municipal auditors, rather than connections with politicians. We also show that local municipal appointments motivate other auditors to become eligible for future drafts, as evidenced by an increase in new professional registrations. Overall, our findings highlight the role of professional experience gained through municipal-auditor service in shaping labor market outcomes within the auditing profession.
